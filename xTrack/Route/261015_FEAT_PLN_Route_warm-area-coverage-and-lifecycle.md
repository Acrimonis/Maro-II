# FEAT_PLN Route — warm area coverage and warm-up lifecycle

- **Feature:** Route
- **Created:** 2026-10-15
- **Status:** the first landing is shipped — the walk, its lifecycle, the store's fullness read, and the review hop's defects fixed — with **D1 (the storage folder) the one question still open** and the rest settled in §8. The ceiling rise and the growth target (§10, §11) are **planned, not built**.
- **Trigger:** the user's requirements list of 2026-10-15, after the fine-tile cache review
- **Scope:** the cache's behaviour end to end — storage, growth, loading and lifecycle. The keying is the accepted foundation, so **which hash to use is not reopened**; the caching around it is.
- **Channel:** the repo rulebook only (no annexe volume applies to this file)

## 1 — What the user asked for

- R1 — Disk persistence keyed on the tile hashes. **In scope: the caching itself. Out of scope: the discussion of which hash to use** — the existing keying is the accepted foundation.
- R2 — That persistence must survive an app restart; the user asks where it actually lives.
- R3 — The cache is built **primarily over one defined area**, and when the size ceiling is not reached it may **auto-extend** west, south, east and north.
- R4 — The cache must stay **lazy, loaded in chunks**.
- R5 — When the defined area is incomplete, warming **starts in a background thread at app start**; if a route acquisition begins while it runs, the warm-up **pauses** until the acquisition is done, then **resumes**.

## 2 — Assessment, requirement by requirement (as it stood before the landing)

This section is the state the work started from; §8 carries what is settled and shipped. Kept because the reasons a decision was taken are the reason it holds.

- **R1 — the persisted layer exists; its behaviour is in scope.** The store names one entry per tile by a SHA-256 of the tile's canonical key bytes, verifies the whole key on load and writes atomically, so a drift can only produce a miss; the hash scheme is settled and not reopened. What stays in scope is everything the cache does around that keying — where it lives, how far it reaches, how it is loaded, and when it is allowed to work.
- **R2 — shipped, but in evictable storage.** The tiles live in the app's private **cache** folder, `/data/user/0/ykws.android.maro/cache/fine_tiles`, installed at the app edge in `MainActivity`. That folder survives a process restart and an app update, so "survive restart" already holds; it does **not** survive the OS reclaiming cache under storage pressure, nor the user's clear-cache. The durable sibling in this app is the private **files** folder, where the coastline data already sits. **Decision needed** (D1).
- **R3 — not shipped; the only genuinely new machinery.** Today there is exactly one fixed area (`route.prewarm.box`) which the pass neither grows nor shrinks, and an area falling outside the baked region is *reported*, never trimmed. Growth is cheap to express because the tile grid is anchored to the world's own lattice, so "rings of tiles outward from the area" is arithmetic; the area only decides which tiles are requested. Two hard stops exist and one watch-out: tiles can only exist where depth and coastline are baked; the disk ceiling caps the store at roughly 220 full tiles (about 350 km² at the 20 m fine cell, fewer when the collar tiles are sparse); and the store trims **oldest-used first**, so a speculative outer ring written today is the first thing reclaimed tomorrow. That last point is D3.
- **R4 — the mechanism exists; the requirement is to prove and tune it.** The directory is never listed at startup, each entry is read on demand, and the tile is the chunk: 64 × 64 fine cells, about 1.3 km a side, roughly 1.6 km²; only the warm pass asks for a listing, and only to skip tiles already warm by name. In scope: proving no other path lists the directory, judging whether the tile is the right chunk, and the cost the review found — every entry stores its whole canonical key beside its members, a large share of a thin tile's file.
- **R5 — half shipped.** The pause half already works: the pass yields before every tile while a route is armed, then resumes to build what it queued. What does **not** match the requirement is the trigger — warming is gated on the *boat being idle*, so on a boat that never idles nothing is ever warmed — and the execution context: the pass today runs inside a composition effect, so its rasterisation runs **on the main thread** block by block, which is the real reason the idle gate looks necessary.

## 3 — The user's two questions, answered

- **"Is idle-only needed if the background work is lower priority than the foreground?"** — A lower priority is worth having and should be added, but it cannot replace the pause gate. The acquisition runs on the engines' own `Dispatchers.Default` scope, and coroutines carry no priority, so a computing warm-up takes a pool worker and slows the arm; the two jobs also share the tile map, whose per-block single-flight is a mutex, so they contend rather than merely compete. Priority lowers how much the phone stutters; the pause is what bounds the interference deterministically, and it is also what keeps a route responsive. On Android the practical levers are a dedicated dispatcher with a background thread priority and limited parallelism, not a per-coroutine setting.
- **"What does 'growth only works if it follows the boat' need?"** — Four things, and no change to the store itself: an area derived from the current position rather than a constant; a cadence for re-deriving it, since re-deriving on every position tick would only re-list and re-key for nothing; a growth rule expressed in tiles outward, with a stop at the ceiling or the baked region's edge; and acceptance that trimming by age then works *for* you, retiring water you have left behind. **The rolling area itself was settled against (D3), and the maximum radius this answer proposed was dropped with it** — the shipped walk has no radius. The keys, the tiles and the ceiling are all position-independent, which is what makes this a policy change rather than a format change.

## 4 — The questions as they stood before implementation (superseded by §8)

Kept for the reasoning behind each. The settled state is §8's alone, and D1 is the only question still open.

- **D1 — storage.** Keep the tiles in the cache folder, or move them to the private files folder. Cache keeps the OS free to reclaim them (correct for a derived cache, wrong if the user expects them to persist across a long trip); files makes them durable and puts them outside every cache-clear. Migration is trivial in code, one install path, and the existing entries are simply orphaned.
- **D2 — what bounds a warm-up.** Today: a fixed 400 tiles and 120 s, both undocumented as measured. The candidate replacement is "until the area is warm, or the store is full", which needs the pass to be able to ask the store how full it is — the running byte total exists inside the store but is not exposed. If the count cap is dropped, a time guard should stay: a ceiling-driven budget on a fast phone is a very long background job. **Settled 2026-10-15: the count cap is kept** — `route.prewarm.maxTiles` bounds the tiles one pass may build *and* the rings the outward walk may take, so a world that names no baked region cannot grow for ever; the time budget stays beside it and the store's own ceiling (step 3) is the third stop.
- **D3 — is growth speculative or rolling.** Growing outward from a fixed area and never moving it wastes the ceiling on water that will be reclaimed before it is used; a rolling area around the boat makes trimming an ally. The two are not exclusive: the fixed area seeds the first run and the rolling radius does the rest.
- **D4 — the trigger, and the indicator.** Warming from app start means giving the pass its own lifecycle instead of the idle signal, and it means the routing square's indicator (which the pass holds down for its whole duration) would light during ordinary app use. Whether that is wanted is a user-visible choice, not an implementation detail.
- **D5 — the dispatcher.** Move the pass off the main thread onto a dedicated, low-priority, limited-parallelism dispatcher. This is a technical choice with no visible effect and can be taken as such.
- **D6 — what else may pause the warm-up.** The pause today keys on a route being armed, but a heavy foreground job the user started — a raster regeneration, a track import — competes for the same CPU to the same effect. The question is whether the pass pauses on any such job or only on a route.
- **D7 — the in-memory ceiling.** Leave the 8 MiB map ceiling alone: a memory miss falls back to the store and costs IO, not a rebuild, so raising it buys latency with heap. Revisit only if the audit shows a memory miss becoming a repeated store read inside a user-facing frame.

## 5 — Plan — the first landing

Steps 1 to 9 are done; step 10 is the user's own. The work added after the landing — the ceiling and the growth target — carries its own steps in §10 and §11 rather than being folded back into this list.

- **1 — settle D1 and D4** (the two user-facing choices) and record them here.
- **2 — audit what the cache does today**, since the caching is in scope: which path lists the directory and when, what one entry costs on disk beside its members, how many entries the ceiling really holds at the working cell size, and whether any read path makes a user-facing frame wait on the store. One divergence to record while doing it: the box named in `AppConfig`'s comment is not the value shipped in the properties file, so the properties file — not the comment — is the home any new value must follow.
- **3 — expose the store's fullness**: a read on the tile-store seam returning the bytes held, fed by the running total the directory store already keeps, so policy can stop on the ceiling instead of a magic count. The read must answer **unknown** rather than zero where there is no store or the total is unprimed — the seam's default is a no-op store and the running total starts unprimed, and a policy reading either as empty would loop forever.
- **4 — make the area a policy object**: the defined area as the seed, growth by tiles outward, the baked-region clamp, and the stop rules (ceiling reached, the region's own edges, the pass's tile budget). Growth is not linear in area: a ring yields tiles only where the active plan keeps fine water, so the policy must count what the plan's own request derivation returns, not what the ring covers.
- **5 — move the pass to a dedicated dispatcher at a lower priority than the foreground** (D5), leaving the store's own IO where it is. Off the screen's thread is half of the requirement; the other half is that its worker runs at a **lower CPU priority** than the app's own work, with limited parallelism, so a prepared water costs the user nothing in smoothness. This comes before widening the trigger on purpose: a pass that leaves the idle window before it leaves the main thread would jank the app's first seconds of ordinary use.
- **6 — give the pass its own lifecycle**: start with the app rather than only on idle, keep the pause on an armed route and the resume afterwards, keep the battery-saver gate, and let the pass be cancelled and resumed without rebuilding what is already warm.
- **7 — decide and implement the indicator behaviour** during a startup warm-up.
- **8 — tests**: gate truth table, growth order and stop on ceiling, skip-when-warm, pause-then-resume, no rasterisation on the main thread, and the store-fullness read.
- **9 — new values go to [`maro.properties`](../../app/src/main/assets/maro.properties)**, one home each, beside the box and the two caps already there: the storage folder choice if any, the growth step, and any retained time guard.
- **10 — device pass is the user's**: a cold start on warm water, a cold start on cold water, and an acquisition started mid-warm-up.

## 6 — Out of scope

- The **choice** of hash, the key's field list, the codec's version and the on-disk entry shape — accepted as they stand (R1), not reopened.
- The extraction of the fine layer itself, and any change to what a tile contains.

## 7 — Risks and objections

- **The strongest objection to the whole warm-up** is that it only pays on water you sail twice: a one-way trip gets nothing from it while the battery is spent on speculative water. That is exactly what a rolling area answers, and it cannot answer the first pass over new water.
- **The strongest objection to rolling growth** is that it does not help the first route of a trip: the water ahead is cold the moment it is first needed, exactly as today, and the warm-up can only ever be ahead of a boat that has been parked. What it does buy is the second and later routes over water just travelled.
- **The strongest objection to dropping the count cap** was that the ceiling alone gives no unit of fairness: on a fast phone the store fills in one long sitting, and every idle window becomes a long CPU job. **Answered by D2 — the count cap was kept**, so the objection never had to be met.
- **The strongest objection to keeping the idle gate** was that it is invisible to the user and simply means the feature is off whenever the boat is moving. **Resolved — the gate is gone** (D4), and with the pass on its own worker the objection it rested on no longer stands.
- **The claim that the pass rasterised on the main thread** was read off the call chain, not measured on device, and it was the state *before* the change: the pass now runs on its own worker, so what the device pass in step 10 still confirms is the absence of jank rather than the presence of the defect.

## 8 — The decisions settled, and what landed (2026-10-15)

- **The walk is outward from the seed** — [`WarmArea`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/spatial/multipass/FineTileWarm.kt) and [`warmOutward`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/spatial/multipass/FineTileWarm.kt): the box is the seed, a step widens it by `route.prewarm.growthStepTiles` tiles of the plan's own lattice, and a tile's side is the builder's own answer ([`fineTileSideOver`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/spatial/multipass/RouteGridBuilder.kt)) rather than arithmetic re-derived at the app edge. The stops are the store's ceiling, the baked region's own edges and the pass's caps — **there is no radius** (D3).
- **D1 — the only decision still open**, and deliberately so: the store still lives in the private **cache** folder, where [`MainActivity`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/MainActivity.kt) installs it, which is the status quo kept rather than a decision taken. Moving it to the durable files folder remains this plan's open question.
- **D2 — settled: the count cap is kept.** `route.prewarm.maxTiles` bounds both the tiles one pass may build and the rings the walk may take, so a world that names no baked region cannot grow for ever; the time budget stays beside it, and the store's own ceiling (step 3) is the third stop.
- **D3 — settled: outward from the fixed seed, not rolling.** Trimming by age then retires the water behind the boat on its own, and the rolling area §3 described is not built.
- **D4 — settled: the pass starts with the app and the dot is the walk's building.** The idle gate is gone; the routing square's dot is lit for each tile the walk really builds — merged across them by the holder's grace, and **dark while the walk waits at its gate**, so an armed route reads as waiting rather than as work — and only where a ring will really build, so a fully warm cache starts a walk that shows no dot at all. **Narrowed by the user on 2026-10-15:** the dot means the warm-up's own work and nothing else, so the map no longer feeds the window per lookup and an arm's store reads can never light it.
- **D5 — settled: the pass has a dispatcher of its own**, bounded by `route.prewarm.threads` and carrying a background CPU priority; the store's own IO stays where it was, and nothing inside `spatial/multipass` adds a hop of its own.
- **D6 — settled: a competing heavy foreground job pauses the pass too** (a depth-raster regeneration), beside an armed route. Battery-saver is the one term that is read per tile and **stops** the pass rather than pausing it.
- **D7 — settled: the in-memory map ceiling is left alone** at 8 MiB.
- **The box divergence step 2 recorded is closed** — [`AppConfig`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/config/AppConfig.kt)'s comment no longer names a value of its own; the shipped box lives in `maro.properties` alone.
- **The review hop's defects, fixed after the first implementation:** the walk now asks what a ring will really build before it holds a window ([`FineTilePrewarm.pending`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/spatial/multipass/FineTilePrewarm.kt)); the battery gate is re-read per tile and ends the pass; the store's ceiling verdict is a live reading rather than a latch ([`DirectoryFineTileStore`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/spatial/DirectoryFineTileStore.kt)); the walk takes one process-level slot and an install no longer zeroes a live walk's hold ([`FineTileCache`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/spatial/multipass/FineTileCache.kt)); and `AppConfig`'s pre-warm bounds are named constants with a doc each. The tests that pin them: the fully warm walk opening no dot, two walks never building at once, a shut gate stopping a pass, the verdict clearing once the directory has room, and a behavioural pin that every build runs on the caller's own dispatcher. The old source-reading dispatch pin is gone — a rename of the hop passed it and it asserted no behaviour at all.

## 9 — The growth stop, and what protects the seed (settled 2026-10-15)

- **The behaviour the user wants is the behaviour that shipped** — the walk grows outward only while there is room and **stops** when there is none; it is not a rolling window and it never grows for the sake of growing.
- **Growth itself evicts nothing.** The walk asks the store before every tile and stops when one more entry of the size just written would not fit, so the pass leaves the store under its ceiling rather than pushing it over.
- **What protects the seed is use, not position.** The user's own reading is the design's: the seed box is the water most likely to be routed over, and a served tile is touched on load, so the box stays fresh as long as it is sailed; only water left alone long enough ages out.
- **The residual, stated rather than hidden** — a single save can overshoot the ceiling by one entry and trim the oldest, and a seed square nobody has routed over for long enough is the first candidate for the same reason. That is the LRU policy doing what it says, and the only cost is a rebuild the next time the water is needed.
- **No code change follows from this section** — it closes the objection raised on 2026-10-15 and records the agreement.

## 10 — The disk ceiling rises to 72 MB (planned 2026-10-15)

- **The decision:** `route.fineTile.maxBytes` moves 48 MiB → 72 MiB. The seed box is about 295 km² and costs well under 42 MB even if every square carried water, so 72 MB holds the box and a generous ring around it — on the same arithmetic, about 330 saturated squares, roughly 540 km² — instead of the box and little else.
- **One home, and the mirrors.** The value's one home is the property in [`maro.properties`](../../app/src/main/assets/maro.properties); [`AppConfig`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/config/AppConfig.kt)'s default and its KDoc mirror it, and [`DirectoryFineTileStore.DEFAULT_MAX_BYTES`](../../app/src/main/java/ykws/android/maro/spatial/DirectoryFineTileStore.kt) — the test-only stand-in — mirrors it too, all three moving in step.
- **What does not move:** no key is added, no tile changes shape, the trim and the once-guarded reclaim alert are untouched; a larger ceiling only delays the first reclamation.
- **Steps.** 1 — update the property and its comment. 2 — update the `AppConfig` default and the number its doc names. 3 — update the store's own mirroring constant. 4 — sweep for any other statement of the old figure. 5 — rebuild, run the unit suite `:app:testDebugUnitTest :app:assembleDebug`, repackage with `apk-build.bat`.
- **No device evidence is owed for this one** — a ceiling is a value, not a reading (R97); the warm-up's own pass stays owed for the behaviour it already carries.
- **A historical line stays as it stands:** the 2026-10-14 plan records the ceiling rising 16 → 48 MiB in its own words, and that record is not rewritten by this section.

## 11 — The growth target: 80 % of the ceiling (settled 2026-10-15)

- **The decision:** the warm area grows only until the store holds **80 % of its ceiling**. The other 20 % is a reserve for water outside the seed area, so an occasional off-area spot is written into free space and evicts nothing.
- **What it fixes:** growth today stops with about one square of room, so the first route over new water saves, trims and evicts — and with a near-first build order the seed is the oldest water there is. At 72 MB the reserve is about 14 MB, roughly 65 saturated squares, near 110 km², which is many trips' worth at a few squares each.
- **What it does not fix, said plainly:** the reserve is consumed, not permanent. Once it is spent, the next save evicts again by the store's own oldest-used-first rule, so this is breathing room rather than immunity; the plan keeps that objection on the record instead of closing it.
- **The implementation shape:** the growth policy asks the store's **fullness** verdict against the target instead of the ceiling — `held + the last entry's size > ceiling × target` — which keeps the existing property of never overshooting and needs no new store read. The target is one new property (`route.prewarm.targetPct`), read through `AppConfig` and clamped on **named bounds** beside the other pre-warm constants — a floor that keeps growth useful and a top below 100 so a reserve always survives; the pass's own cap and the store's hard ceiling stay exactly as they are.
- **It is the rule at every start, not a one-off:** the walk recomputes its rings each session, so without this rule a later start would simply fill the reserve.
- **Rejected and parked rather than built.** A distance-weighted eviction — oldest **and** farthest — answers a real inversion, since pure recency evicts the seed first, but it needs position at trim time, which the frozen hash naming and the one-file-one-entry read keep out of reach, plus two weights that nothing measured can price. Protecting the seed area outright is the stronger guarantee and stays open beside it, needing only the seed's bounds and one trim rule.
- **Owed from this thread:** the ceiling itself (72 MiB, §10), this target, and the warm-up's own device pass — none of them implemented yet, and none of them needing device evidence but the last.
