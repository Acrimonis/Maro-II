# Route — the corridor tracer (the taut-string engine), as it was

**Date:** 2026-09-22 · **Status:** retired and removed — the code behind it is deleted by [`260922_FEAT_PLN_Route_dummy-engine-and-engine-removal.md`](260922_FEAT_PLN_Route_dummy-engine-and-engine-removal.md)
**Provenance:** read off the eleven sources in `spatial/taut/` before their deletion, and off the design of record that carried its measurements — [`260920_FEAT_PLN_Route_taunt_algorithm-design.md`](260920_FEAT_PLN_Route_taunt_algorithm-design.md), §19 in particular — plus [`260920_FEAT_PLN_Route_engine-seam-and-parking.md`](260920_FEAT_PLN_Route_engine-seam-and-parking.md). Both are archived on 2026-09-22.

## 1. What it was

- A **taut string over live geometry**: the shortest-clock line that the water, the shore offset and the priced zones allow, drawn on a visibility graph whose nodes are the corners a taut line can bend on. It needed **no bake and no new artifact** — every input it read was already loaded by the app — and it replaced the mesh engine as the design of record on 2026-09-20, switched in at one expression in `MapScreen`.

## 2. Flow

1. **Corridor.** `TautGraph.corridorBox` sized a box from the two ends — 1 NM × 2^growth plus the berth — and the search ran inside it. An empty answer grew the box once and re-ran, and a second empty answer was a **named refusal** (`TautRefusal`), never a silent nothing. The box was then re-sized from the obstacles it must contain (`sizedFromObstacles`, clamped and inflated by the berth) and re-harvested per growth attempt.
2. **Terrain, harvested once per corridor.** `TautObstacles.harvest` took the land polylines and the **2 m contour** where the soundings are fine, simplified each **one-sided and outward** at a stated fraction of the berth, then dilated by the berth and applied the shore offset — so the abstraction could only ever delete water, never create it, and every span was validated by **exact segment distance** against the undilated ring. `TautGraph.harvestZones` built the `TautZoneSet` (outer rings, holes, `covers` as "outer minus holes", `interiorSpans` as one interval per leg, and the berth's price rising as the boundary nears, ceilinged at twice a leg's own time). `TautBand` turned the 300 m band from a midpoint boolean into a **span source**, its crossing closed in closed form.
3. **Graph.** Vertices were the frozen start, the resolved aim, every corner of every wall that **protrudes into the water**, and every such corner of every zone ring and hole. An edge existed where the line between two vertices crossed no wall and its own middle stood on traversable water; its price was the **clock alone** summed over the leg's own limit regions. A **pace and a world** entered nowhere but the price, so the corner-to-corner edge set could be kept as `TautGraphBase` and reused while the next corridor lay inside the kept terrain — only the two ends' rows were rebuilt per search (O(N) against O(N²)).
4. **Search.** `TautSearch` ran an A\* whose state carried the **arrival direction**, so a corner could be priced at all, with a stated tie-break (shorter, then fewer turns) and never insertion order. It ran **two runs**: priced zones forbidden first and, only when that found nothing, priced and taken with the crossing reported by name. At every corner it fitted an **easing spiral** where the water allowed — `√(a·r)` with the lateral cap — and charged the longitudinal brake-and-accelerate term otherwise.
5. **Clock and output.** The drawn line was **cut where the limit changes**, through the same splitter the price reads, so the trip figure, the panel and the saved course all describe the line on the screen. `RouteResult.Success` carried the polyline, the per-leg times, the length and the duration; the tracer's own dossier rode beside it.

## 3. Limitations and issues

- **Cost was quadratic in the corridor's corners before anything was asked of the graph** — every pair of vertices was tested for visibility and priced: **92 665 pairs** at 431 vertices on the acceptance pair. The keep-the-base lever cut the *rebuild*, not the pair scan.
- **The easing fit dominated the search.** On the acceptance pair the search spent **95 % of its time at the per-corner door**: 6 274 fits inside 792 ms, and inside the fit **9 798 candidate arcs** cost 19 ms to draw against **652 ms to judge**. The lever was the water judge, not the curve.
- **The levers were measured and mostly refused.** A memoised judge was built and measured, then its gain **withdrawn on its own reading** (an 8–35 % hit rate); three cheaper water questions were priced and two refused; the candidate that moved an answer was refused on the six-metric guard; the narrower corridor was refused on a hunted guard (3 of 3 candidates answered at 1.0 NM with 2 routes lost by narrowing); the cheaper point question was priced and refused (the coast index's walk is 97 % of `isWater`'s 441 ms).
- **Corridor sufficiency is an assumption, not a proof.** A route that must leave the box is simply not found, and inflate-and-re-run is a policy rather than a guarantee.
- **The abstraction's tolerance is a ceiling as much as a value** — a harvested corner stands `berth − tolerance` off the obstacle it came from, so a tolerance of one berth makes the engine's own guarantee vacuous.
- **Two inshore pairs never routed**, and the reason was the water rather than the corridor: their coordinates resolve **onto land** (both starts, and one aim), so the engine refused them by name — `START_OFF_WATER`, `AIM_OFF_WATER`.
- **The refusal's name reached no user.** `START_OFF_WATER` and its kin warned to the log and returned an empty answer, which every surface reads as an ordinary empty draft — a named reason the design itself required, spoken only to a log reader. It stayed open when the work stopped.
- **The wall set was not reproducible across JVM launches.** A contour line's dominant source was broken by a tie in `HashMap<DepthSource, Int>` iteration order over an enum key, so the harvested walls — and a route's own line — could move between runs.
- **A reused answer may differ from a cold one**, by construction: the kept box makes the graph a superset, and the drag reading measured it at 0.00 m from the cold line on four of six aims and **19.42 m on two**, dearer by 0.01 s on three. The guard was therefore re-scoped from "the same line" to "never dearer".
- **Smaller defects already repaired, kept here because they are the class of thing to expect:** the leg's limit read at its midpoint (which let a leg straddling the 300 m line carry one limit over the change); a corner clip that crossed a zone ring twice and read as *not entered*; a sharp corner priced **free** rather than charged; and a graph build that could hang.
- **Five code-health observations were recorded and never closed**: `SharpCause` misnamed, a second `landPolylinesIn` per build, the grid walk now serving two readers, `berthFraction`'s interval exclusion dearer than the point read it replaced, and `regionsOf` naming a leg's far end as itself.

## 4. Performance

- **Desktop, the acceptance pair (Baie des Milliardaires → Port de la Salis), split into its three phases:** harvest **280 ms** · graph **138 ms** · search **1 003 ms** (wall 1 423 ms) over 431 vertices, 3 682 edges and 92 665 pairs. The answer: **63 vertices · 524° of turn · 52° worst corner · 5.35 km · ETA 25.26 min**, rising to **27.40 min** once the band's limit was priced honestly per piece (the search choosing a different line, not the band re-pricing one that was already down).
- **On the phone (Pixel 7, debug APK, two captures):** the same pair's corridor read harvest **2 671 ms** · graph **4 816 ms** · search **56 079 ms** — **~54× the desktop**, with the search keeping its 95 %-per-corner shape, over 425 vertices, 3 493 edges and 90 100 pairs. The two phases that are *not* the search are where the reuse paid: with the graph kept beside the terrain, a repeat search read graph **85 → 1 ms** over 92 665 → 859 pairs, harvest 58 → 0 ms and wall 753 → 160 ms.
- **The user's own target was never set**: the phone's acceptable route latency was the plan's first item and the gate on the rest, and it was still open when the work stopped. The feature's own stated target had been moved to 3 000 ms of wall clock for that pair, and the phone missed it by an order of magnitude.
- **No release measurement exists.** The module ships no release build type and no signing config, so every figure above is a **debug** APK's, and the two readings that are not timings (degenerate previews where the aim never left the start's corner, and the judge's tallies riding a kept terrain) were named so a later run could not misread them.

## 5. Why it went

- The user's verdict was on the result, not on the theory: **"this algo doesn't work"** — the line still did not read as a usable route, and the cost on the device made the preview unusable at exactly the moment it is asked for.
- The open points were all of the same kind — a corridor that may be too small, a tolerance that trades the guarantee for speed, a refusal no user ever sees, a non-reproducible wall set — and each of them was a *decision about the algorithm's shape* rather than a bug to fix. Removing it and leaving the seam is the cheaper way to re-open that question.
